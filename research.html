Working Papers

Nonparametric Test for the Equality of Conditional Distributions in Selection Models
With Cristine Pinto and Luis Alvarez — WP, 2026
Empirical research in economics relies on assumptions such as exogeneity and correct model specification to justify causal interpretation and identification. In practice, these assumptions are typically assessed through restrictions on conditional means, due to their analytical and computational convenience. However, such approaches are inherently limited: violations may arise through higher-order moments of the distribution without affecting conditional means, rendering mean-based tests asymptotically powerless. This paper develops a nonparametric framework for testing equality of conditional distributions in selection models. The proposed hypothesis is fully distributional and encompasses implications of exogeneity and correct specification as special cases. The method is based on the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD). We show that existing MMD baed tests are invalid under irregular selection mechanisms, leading to size distortions. To address this, we introduce a regime-specific smoothness condition that ensures valid asymptotic inference. For global inference, we combine local test statistics using a Poisson-Binomial distribution, which accommodates heterogeneous behavior across the covariate space. The proposed test is consistent, and achieves the optimal local detection rate. Simulation results confirm good size and power properties.

Work in Progress

Confidence Sets for Identified Sets via Classification-Based Metropolis-Hastings
In many econometric applications, particularly in discontinuous structural models, identification relies on restricted features of the data distribution (moment conditions), often due to the intractability of the likelihood in complex models. These approaches may fail to fully exploit the available information in the data, leading to outer identified sets rather than sharper identification. This project proposes a likelihood-based approach for settings with intractable likelihoods, leveraging recent advances in likelihood-free inference. By combining classification methods with a Metropolis–Hastings algorithm, we recover an implicit likelihood and draw samples from the posterior distribution. Confidence sets for the identified set are then constructed using quantiles of the sample classifier criterion evaluated at these draws. The approach extends naturally to inference on subvectors of parameters and provides a computationally attractive alternative to existing methods. The method is particularly well-suited to discontinuous structural models, such as entry games with multiple equilibria, where likelihood intractability and non-convexity are inherent features of the problem.
